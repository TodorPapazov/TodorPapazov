Origin diversity and community outcomes

This page rates how widely the people who were not born in a community come from different origin communities. It does that once for US states, and once for communities outside the United States. The same rule scores every place. The two sets are ranked only within themselves.

The rating rule

The rating uses only people who were not born in that community, and how those people are spread across origin communities in the published table. The same newcomer count from one origin scores lower than that count spread across many origins. A community with no such newcomers scores 0, the bottom of the scale. One origin scores 1. An even split across four origins scores 4. A lopsided mix of the same people scores lower than an even mix. Scoring the same counts again returns the same rating.

The score is the effective number of origin communities: the exponential of the Shannon entropy of the origin shares. Order does not matter. People born in the community are not in the count.

For a US state, the people in the rating are the foreign-born population in the 2019-2023 American Community Survey: people born outside the United States. An origin is a Census place-of-birth line. A named country counts as one origin. A residual line such as Other Eastern Africa also counts as one origin, because that is how the Census groups people it does not assign to a named country. A detail already included in a country line, such as the Azores inside Portugal, is not added again. People born in another US state are not in this rating.

For a community outside the United States, the people in the rating are the 2024 UN international migrant stock by country or area of origin. That stock is the foreign-born population where the country publishes birthplace, and the foreign-citizen count where it does not. People from the community itself are not included. The residual origin Others counts as one origin. Regional totals are not added on top of the country lines. The United States and US territories are left out of this set.

US states, including the District of Columbia, are ranked only among themselves. Communities outside the United States are ranked only among themselves. Rank 1 is the highest rating inside that set. There is no combined league table.

US states

Among US states, the direction is positive: higher origin diversity sits with higher median household income. Spearman rank correlation is 0.151674 across 51 communities. This describes the published numbers. It does not show that people born elsewhere caused the outcome.

Ohio has the highest rating in this set (63.4599), with 593106 people born elsewhere. California has more people born elsewhere (10479497) and a lower rating (19.2410). The rating follows how those people are spread, not how many of them there are.

The outcome is median household income from the same 2019-2023 American Community Survey, in 2023 dollars.

Communities outside the United States

Among communities outside the US, the direction is positive: higher origin diversity sits with higher GDP per capita. Spearman rank correlation is 0.417439 across 201 communities. This describes the published numbers. It does not show that people born elsewhere caused the outcome.

Denmark has the highest rating in this set (59.3785), with 847475 people born elsewhere. Germany has more people born elsewhere (16750084) and a lower rating (17.6274). The rating follows how those people are spread, not how many of them there are.

The outcome is World Bank GDP per capita in current US dollars. The year is 2023 when that figure is published, and 2022 when it is not. The year is in the table.
